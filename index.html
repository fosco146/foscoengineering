Results

Finding money the firm had already earned

A national electrical testing firm

Problem
Two kinds of revenue were stuck. Some projects were fully complete but never fully invoiced. Others were partially done and had stalled, so they never reached the finish line or the invoice. No one had a single view of which jobs were in which state.
Fix
A live pipeline in the firm's existing database showing every project's status, completion and billing state, with flags for finished-but-unbilled work and stalled jobs.
Result
$250K+ in earned revenue identified, completed and invoiced, and a shorter cycle from field work to invoice.

Two hours back on every report

Same firm, 100+ technical reports a year

Problem
Every report was hand-built. Staff re-typed data from the database into Word, formatted tables by hand and assembled the final PDF package by drag-and-drop. Then a reviewer checked it all for copy errors.
Fix
A one-click report template that pulls data straight from the database, plus a desktop tool that assembles the client package automatically.

Common questions

What kinds of businesses do you work with?

Owner-run trade and service businesses with roughly 5–50 people: HVAC, plumbing, electrical, fire and security, field service, facility maintenance, small manufacturers and property managers.

Do I have to buy new software?

Usually not. I start with the tools you already have, like your spreadsheets, accounting software or job management system, and only recommend something new when it clearly pays for itself.

Why fixed prices instead of hourly?

You know the cost before we start, and I'm paid for results, not time. Every build is scoped in writing first.

Do you work remotely?

Yes. I meet in person in the Boston area when it helps, and everything else works remotely.

How long does it take?

The audit takes two weeks. Most builds take two to six weeks depending on scope.

Where does AI fit in?

Where it saves real time, like reading documents, drafting follow-ups or sorting requests. I don't add AI for its own sake.
